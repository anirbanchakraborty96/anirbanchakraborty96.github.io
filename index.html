Research

Reading the tissue response, gene by gene

Implanted microelectrodes lose signal over weeks to months, and the tissue around them is a large part of the reason. My work asks which cells change, which genes drive that change, and how far the effect reaches from the device — using spatial and single-cell transcriptomics rather than a handful of stained markers.

Day to day that means building analysis pipelines for 10x Genomics Visium and Xenium datasets, running statistical and network models across cell types, implant materials, brain regions and time points, and turning the result into something a neuroengineer can act on. Reactivity markers like Gfap, C1qa, C3 and Clu are the recurring characters.

I came to this from biomedical engineering and hardware, so I care about both ends of the problem: the biology at the interface, and the electrode that provoked it.

Software · published

SpatiaXen

An interactive, no-code platform for cell-type-resolved analysis of Xenium spatial transcriptomics data — built so wet-lab researchers can explore their own slides without writing a pipeline. Presented as an invited talk at ISMB 2026.

Method · published

DiffCoRank

A framework for finding hub genes and differential co-expression networks in brain implant-associated tissue, so that the molecular drivers of the response can be ranked rather than eyeballed. Published in BMC Bioinformatics.

Ongoing

Material and time-course comparisons

Systematic single-cell-resolution comparison of the tissue response to silicon and polyimide electrodes at sub- and supra-cellular scale, across acute to chronic time points.
